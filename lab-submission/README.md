**Student:** Ainat Yermurat | **ID:** 230103161

The experiments identify three main performance bottlenecks on the tested machine: poor memory locality, shared-memory contention, and multiprocessing overhead. In the stride benchmark, access cost increased from **1.079 ns/element** at stride 1 to **4.039 ns/element** at stride 8 and **12.139 ns/element** at stride 128, demonstrating the loss of cache-line utilization. Loop interchange improved dense-kernel runtime from **1.756 s** (i-j-k) to **1.552 s** (i-k-j), with a maximum numerical difference of **8.53 × 10⁻¹³**. In the false-sharing experiment, the unpadded runtime rose from **0.598 s** at 1 process to **1.780 s** at 8 processes, and its Karp–Flatt metric reached **e(8) = 3.258**, indicating severe contention and coordination costs; padding reduced the eight-process runtime to **1.667 s** but did not restore ideal scaling, while privatization reduced it to **0.461 s**. Strong scaling of the stencil peaked at 2 processes (**0.247 s → 0.222 s**) and deteriorated at 8 processes (**0.371 s**), whereas weak-scaling runtime increased from **0.249 s** to **0.922 s** as the workload grew. Overall, contiguous access and private accumulation improved performance, but shared-memory coherence and process-management costs limited parallel speedup.
